Syllabus

Course Information

MATH 435: Mathematical Imaging

(3 Lecture hours, 0 Laboratory hours, 3 Credits)

Prerequisites

  • (MATH 231 or MATH 232), MATH 315, and one of MATH 242, MATH 243, or MATH 244

Course Catalog Description

This course introduces the mathematical foundations of imaging science from an inverse problems perspective. It covers image formation, reconstruction, and degradation using tools from linear algebra, Fourier analysis, probability, and optimization. Regularization methods and computational techniques for stable image reconstruction are studied. Applications focus on medical and scientific imaging.

Textbook

  • M. Bertero, P. Boccacci, and C. De Mol, Introduction to Inverse Problems in Imaging, 2nd ed. Boca Raton, FL, USA: CRC Press, 2021.

Reference Materials

  • A. Chambolle and T. Pock, “An introduction to continuous optimization for imaging,” Acta Numerica, vol. 25, pp. 161–319, 2016.

  • S. Mallat, A Wavelet Tour of Signal Processing, 3rd ed. Burlington, MA, USA: Academic Press, 2009.

  • L. Najman, Mathematical Imaging, online course book for MATH 435.

Course Structure & Learning Methodology

Lectures are 2 × 75 minutes per week. Lectures are presented using slides and whiteboard. The course is facilitated by computational demonstrations. A team modeling project is conducted throughout the semester to reinforce understanding.

Instructor Policy on Late Submission of Assignments

Quizzes • There will be no make-up quizzes. If the student fails to attend a scheduled quiz without an approved absence from the Student Success Department, then a grade of zero for that quiz will be assigned.

• If the student fails to attend a quiz and the Student Success Department confirms that the student’s absence from that quiz is approved, then the grade that will be assigned to that quiz is

– the grade of the Semester examination if that quiz took place before the Semester examination;

– the grade of the Final Examination if that quiz took place after the Semester examination.

Semester examination • There will be no make-up Semester Examination. If the student fails to attend the scheduled Semester examination without an approved absence from the Student Success Department, then a grade of zero will be assigned for the Semester examination.

• If the student fails to attend the Semester examination and the Student Success Department confirms that the absence from the Semester examination is approved, then the grade for the Final Examination will also be assigned as the grade for the Semester examination.

A maximum of 40% of the course grade can be compensated through the above policy

Additional information

Assessment

Late submission of the group project will not be accepted.

Project

The project will be assigned in Week 6 of the semester. Students will work in pairs (teams of two) to formulate, analyze, and solve a mathematical imaging reconstruction problem. Each team will develop an appropriate mathematical model, implement computational reconstruction methods, analyze the results, and evaluate model performance and parameter sensitivity.

A brief, ungraded progress draft is due in Week 8. It should identify the forward model, proposed reconstruction method and initial computational results. Feedback will be provided by Week 9 to guide the final report and code.

Students are required to use Python for all computational implementations and analyses.

A written report and the corresponding code must be submitted in Week 14.

In Week 15, each team will participate in an individual oral presentation. Each student will be examined separately on the modeling decisions, implementation, and interpretation of results. Both students are expected to demonstrate full understanding of the entire project.

Failure to participate in the oral presentation will result in a maximum of 50% of the project grade.

Students may use computational tools and AI assistance for the project, but must be able to justify and defend all modeling decisions during the oral presentation.
